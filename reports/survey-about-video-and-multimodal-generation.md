# Video and Multimodal Generation: Architectures, Unified Models, Evaluation, and Control

## TL;DR

- Latent video diffusion with Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbones is the dominant paradigm: Latte tokenizes video into spatio-temporal tokens and models their distribution in latent space [1], CogVideoX scales the recipe to 10-second, 16 fps, 768*1360 clips with a 3D VAE and an "expert transformer" for text-video alignment [2], and HunyuanVideo and Wan package the same paradigm as open foundation models whose headlines are data curation, pre-training strategy, and automated metrics rather than a new backbone [3][4].
- Discrete-token autoregression is the older, still-live alternative: Phenaki compresses video into discrete tokens with a causal-in-time tokenizer to support variable length [5] and VideoPoet trains a decoder-only transformer over images, video, text and audio under the LLM pre-train-then-adapt protocol [6]; by 2025 the autoregressive idea survives mainly as streaming or compressed-context long-video diffusion such as Rolling Forcing [7] and LoViC [8].
- Designs genuinely disagree about where temporal structure should be handled: Lumiere generates the entire duration in a single Space-Time U-Net pass specifically to avoid keyframe-plus-temporal-super-resolution pipelines [9], Latte compares temporal-attention variants within a latent transformer [1], and long-horizon work attacks error accumulation and quadratic attention directly [7][8].
- Evaluation has shifted from single scores to decomposed, prompt-tailored dimensions: VBench motivates itself on the claim that existing metrics do not fully align with human perception [10], EvalCrafter rejects judging large video models by FVD or IS alone [11], and later benchmarks isolate compositionality [12] and temporal compositionality across both text-to-video and image-to-video [13].
- Unified any-to-any generation is architecturally diverse but not yet dominant over specialists: it is built on discrete token streams [14][15], shared continuous tokenizers [16], or hybrid tokenizer-plus-diffusion-decoder designs [17], yet benchmarking reports unified models still trailing specialized vision-language models overall [18] and still relying on separate visual representations for understanding and generation [19].

## Background

Video generation entered the transformer era through two distinct doors. The token-based door treated video as a sequence: Phenaki introduced a video representation of a small set of discrete tokens whose tokenizer uses causal attention in time, allowing variable-length videos to be generated from a sequence of textual prompts by a bidirectional masked transformer [5]. VideoPoet pushed the same intuition to its LLM conclusion, using a decoder-only transformer over image, video, text and audio inputs and a mixture of multimodal generative objectives during pre-training [6]. The latent-diffusion door instead kept continuous latents: Latte extracts spatio-temporal tokens from videos and applies transformer blocks to model the video distribution in latent space [1], and Lumiere generates the full temporal duration in one pass through a Space-Time U-Net with both spatial and temporal down/upsampling [9].

Those two lineages now share most of their machinery. CogVideoX compresses video along both spatial and temporal dimensions with a 3D VAE and adds an expert transformer for text-video alignment [2]; Wan likewise foregrounds a novel VAE plus scalable pre-training and data curation [4]. In parallel, the multimodal side of the field stopped treating images as a separate specialization: unified models must decide how text, image, audio and video become one representational currency, which is exactly the decision the token-based video lineage had already faced [5][6].

## Latent video diffusion transformers and scaling recipes

The recipe papers differ mainly in what they hold fixed and what they vary. Latte is an early explicit latent diffusion transformer that compares four variants decomposing the spatial and temporal dimensions of input clips, deriving best practices such as video clip patch embedding and temporal-attention variants [1]. Lumiere answers the same question at the architecture level by removing the cascade: rather than synthesizing distant keyframes and then temporally super-resolving them, it generates the whole duration in a single Space-Time U-Net pass, which the paper argues is necessary for global temporal consistency [9]. CogVideoX sits between these poles, scaling a DiT-based text-to-video model to 10-second, 16 fps, 768*1360 output and attributing the gain to both the 3D VAE's higher compression rate and the expert transformer's text-video alignment [2].

STIV treats architecture, training recipe and data curation as a single joint design problem and conditions a DiT on images by frame replacement alongside joint text conditioning [20]; HunyuanVideo and Wan generalize that into "system" papers, presenting open video foundation models whose contributions are framework-level (data pipelines, scalable pre-training, automated evaluation metrics) rather than a single module [3][4]. The one quantitative scaling result in this set is that DiT pre-training loss follows a power law in compute over budgets from 1e17 to 6e18 FLOPs for both image and video generation, which the authors present as the first confirmation of DiT scaling laws [21]. Notably, the retrieved evidence supports convergence on scaling behavior far better than it supports any ranking among backbones: HunyuanVideo claims performance comparable to or surpassing leading closed-source models and Wan highlights its 14B configuration [3][4], but these are qualitative relative claims rather than controlled head-to-head comparisons.

## Autoregressive, token-based and long-horizon generation

The clearest contrast in the retrieved literature is not "diffusion versus autoregression" but "one-shot attention versus causal/compressed context". Phenaki's causal-in-time tokenizer exists because variable-length generation requires causality over time, and its tokens are then produced from text by a bidirectional masked transformer [5]; VideoPoet shows the same multimodal token interface can be trained with standard LLM objectives [6]. The 2025 long-video work returns to causality for a different reason, efficiency and drift: Rolling Forcing targets streaming long video with minimal error accumulation and explicitly does not iteratively sample individual frames, contrasting with frame-by-frame autoregressive sampling [7], while LoViC generates long, coherent video segment-wise and introduces the FlexFormer autoencoder tokenizer to address the quadratic complexity of self-attention, arguing that prior sparse-attention and temporally autoregressive methods compromise either temporal coherence or scalability [8].

Efficiency work on the diffusion-transformer side converges on the same bottleneck from the opposite direction: MC-Sparse frames its problem as the latency of diffusion transformers in long-sequence generation such as video and high-resolution 3D asset generation, traces quality degradation under sparsity to token-grouping constraints, inaccurate interaction selection and lost attention contributions, and proposes a training-free sparse-attention framework [22]. Taken together, the two lineages increasingly differ in their sampling regime (few-pass latent denoising versus causal streaming) rather than in whether they use a transformer over learned visual tokens [7][8][22].

## Unified any-to-any multimodal generation

The unified-generation literature splits into four construction strategies. The fully discrete route tokenizes every modality into one vocabulary and trains a single sequence model, as in AnyGPT, which reports performance on par with specialized models and subsumes speech alongside text and images [14]. The continuous-latent route argues that a shared continuous visual tokenizer avoids the reconstruction cost of discrete codebooks: Ming-UniVision's MingTok unifies vision-language understanding and generation inside one autoregressive framework with reported state-of-the-art results in both domains [16]. The hybrid route separates representation sharing from synthesis sampling, as in MANZANO, which pairs a hybrid vision tokenizer with a diffusion decoder and reports state-of-the-art understanding and generation [17]. The fourth strategy changes the generative process itself: Omni-Diffusion substitutes masked discrete diffusion for left-to-right decoding and is described as the first any-to-any multimodal language model built on mask-based discrete diffusion, covering text, speech and images [15].

Instruction tuning is the common bridge from an understanding-only model to a generating one. MetaMorph's visual-predictive instruction tuning extends visual instruction tuning so that a pre-trained LLM emits visual as well as text tokens, reporting competitive understanding and generation [23]; INSET goes further by embedding images as native vocabulary inside textual instructions so interleaved inputs, generation and editing share one sequence format [24]. The recurring constraint behind both is data: interleaved instruction corpora, rather than model capacity, are what make unified generation scalably trainable [23][24].

## Evaluating video and multimodal generation

Benchmarks have moved from a single fidelity number to decomposed, prompt-tailored dimensions. VBench dissects video generation quality into specific, hierarchical and disentangled dimensions, each with tailored prompts and evaluation methods, and justifies the design by arguing that existing metrics do not fully align with human perception [10]; EvalCrafter makes the same critique of FVD and IS, arguing that models trained on very large datasets with multi-aspect abilities cannot be judged by simple metrics and proposing an exhaustive evaluation pipeline tied to human perception [11]. Compositionality became a benchmark target in its own right: T2V-CompBench is presented as the first benchmark tailored for compositional text-to-video generation, covering aspects such as consistent and dynamic attribute binding and spatial relations [12], while TC-Bench isolates temporal compositionality for both text-to-video and image-to-video, requiring the emergence of new concepts and relation transitions over time rather than only simple actions [13].

Newer work attacks the benchmark methodology itself: WorldJen argues that SSIM and PSNR reward pixel fidelity over semantic correctness, that FVD favors distributional textures over physical plausibility, and that binary-VQA benchmarks such as VBench 2.0 are prone to yes-bias and rely on low-resolution auditors that miss temporal failures, while single-dimension prompts multiply the required video budget without guaranteeing coverage [25]. On the multimodal side, OmniCapBench reframes audio-visual caption evaluation as a deep-structured diagnostic framework, motivated by a coupled trade-off in existing benchmarks where whole-caption scores give coverage without localization, local probes give localization without coverage, and unconstrained LLM judges add instability [26]. The two literature streams therefore share a diagnosis (aggregate metrics and unconstrained judges are unreliable) but have not converged on a single protocol, and the evidence base is thin on image-to-video condition-following specifically, which only TC-Bench covers explicitly [13].

## Control, editing and world models

Controllable generation has converged on one taxonomic distinction, camera motion versus object motion. MotionCtrl makes that distinction its core design and proposes a unified, flexible controller that conditions camera and object motion independently, motivated by the observation that prior work handles one type or blurs them [27]; CameraCtrl isolates the camera axis instead, contributing a camera trajectory parameterization plus a plug-and-play camera-pose control module trained on top of a video diffusion model while leaving the base model's other modules untouched [28]. The two differ in scope, not in goal, and neither abstract specifies how adherence to the requested trajectory is measured, so control-following evaluation remains under-specified relative to the text-to-video benchmark literature [10][27][28].

Editing follows a different data strategy. VINCIE-NExT frames video editing as harder than generation because it requires (source, instruction, edited) triplets that are expensive to scale, whereas image editing has large paired datasets, and it therefore transfers editing capability from images to video through in-context visual demonstrations [29]; FreeMatching similarly targets dense correspondence for image editing and reference-guided generation, where transformations preserve visual identity while breaking physical continuity [30]. Both substitute priors or demonstrations for scarce paired video supervision.

World models are the most active framing in the retrieved trending feed, and they split into embodied control and explorable geometry. ME-World formulates multi-agent egocentric world modeling as synchronized ego-stream generation for multiple agents interacting through fine-grained actions in a shared world, arguing that existing multi-agent world models rely on coarse actions such as locomotion or discrete commands [31]; WorldGuide instead poses procedural generation as closed-loop generation for a goal-directed world model, targeting the gap between deciding an action and successfully realizing it in long-horizon tasks [32]. On the geometric side, OuroWorld converts any static 3D Gaussian Splatting scene into a seamlessly looping 3D cinemagraph by having a vision-language model infer plausible dynamics, guiding a video model to synthesize a reference video that is lifted into multi-view video, and using a Fourier-series deformation field that guarantees looping by construction [33]; SPW-Nav streams one minute of 2K 360-degree video in real time from a single panorama while interpreting each instruction in the previously generated panorama as camera motion [34]. A fifth, orthogonal thread appears in the same feed: reward-based post-training for image generation, where preference rewards trained with a Bradley-Terry objective are composed with rubric-based rewards on the argument that no single reward signal captures the full range of human preference [35].

## Trends and open problems

- **Backbone convergence despite a nominal paradigm split.** Latent DiT diffusion dominates model papers [1][2][3][4][20], but the token-based lineage contributed the ideas (causal-in-time tokenization, mixed multimodal objectives) that now reappear inside streaming and compressed-context video models [5][6][7][8]. The practical frontier is therefore sampling regime and context handling rather than a binary diffusion-versus-autoregression choice.
- **Scaling is now a data and systems story.** The only quantitative scaling claim retrieved is a power-law loss-versus-compute relation for DiTs over 1e17-6e18 FLOPs [21], while the frontier model reports emphasize data curation, VAE design and evaluation tooling [3][4] and one study argues architecture, training recipe and data must be tuned jointly [20]. No retrieved source provides a controlled head-to-head between the two families, so relative superiority remains unestablished.
- **Long-horizon coherence and cost are unsolved.** Both the streaming route [7] and the segment-wise compressed-context route [8] are explicitly motivated by the failure of naive full-sequence attention, and training-free sparse attention is proposed to close the quality gap that sparsity opens in video and 3D generation [22]. Because these abstracts report qualitative gains, the magnitude of the improvement is not verifiable from this evidence set.
- **Evaluation is fragmenting faster than it is consolidating.** Decomposed dimension suites [10], exhaustive human-perception pipelines [11], compositionality [12], temporal compositionality [13] and multi-dimensional diagnostics that critique VQA-based auditing [25] are competing protocols rather than layers of one standard, and audio-visual captioning adds its own coverage-versus-localization trade-off [26]. Image-to-video condition-following is covered explicitly by only one retrieved benchmark [13].
- **Unified models still pay a specialist tax, and the interface is unresolved.** Unified generation spans discrete sequences [14][15], continuous tokenizers [16] and hybrid tokenizer-plus-decoder designs [17], yet evaluation of generation-to-understanding behavior reports overall lag behind specialized vision-language models with only subtask-level gains [18], and the diagnosis is partly architectural: unified models often keep separate visual representations for understanding and generation, inflating visual context length, while video understanding and generation use different temporal representations [19]. Instruction-tuned unification depends on interleaved corpora that are themselves the bottleneck [23][24].
- **Control and editing lack commensurate evaluation.** Motion conditioning is well specified as a mechanism [27][28] but not as a measured capability, and editing work avoids paired-video supervision by transferring image priors or demonstrations [29][30]; world-model entries push the same problem toward closed-loop and long-horizon settings where success criteria are even less settled [31][32][34][33].
- **Evidence limits.** The sources above were retrieved from arXiv listings, Hugging Face paper search and the Hugging Face Daily Papers feed. Daily Papers and paper-search records are abstract-level and, in the retrieved feed, several are dated later than the arxiv entries; claims attributed to them are limited to what those listings state (including feed ranking signals such as upvotes [31]), and no benchmark numbers, author lists or dates beyond the retrieved fields are asserted here. An independent re-fetch of the cited URLs for verification was attempted at the end of this run but every request failed with an HTTP 429 error and the sandbox has no outbound network, so no claim could be re-confirmed or refuted against live pages; the citations therefore rest on the records returned by the three retrieval tools during this run, and abstract-level text that was truncated at retrieval time is the binding limit on specificity.
