# Efficient Inference and Small Language Models: Compression, Decoding, and Serving Trade-offs

## TL;DR

- Two research fronts attack the same cost envelope from opposite ends: making the model smaller before deployment (quantization, pruning, distillation) and making each generated token cheaper at runtime (speculative decoding, cache eviction, context compaction, routing). The retrieved evidence supports strong gains *within* each front, but no retrieved result compares the fronts head to head [1][2][3][4][5].
- Post-training quantization reports the most aggressive operating points: rate-distortion-optimal weights at 3-5 bits per scalar with KV values down to 1.7 bits, benchmarked against named quantization baselines [1], and a staged mixed-precision scheme averaging roughly 1.6 bits per weight by keeping 80% of weights at 1 bit [6].
- Small models are as often *produced* as trained: task-scoped students are distilled from large teachers [7][8][9], variable-size families are initialized through chains of small anchor models to avoid repeated teacher access [10], and structured pruning compresses existing checkpoints into smaller ones [11][12][13].
- Inference-time memory economy has become a sub-field of its own: KV eviction is now derived from an exact eviction-error factorization [14], retention patterns are learned over layer, head and relative-distance [15], and agent history is compacted into bounded soft memory tokens on a frozen model [16].
- Reported sizes are heterogeneous, which blocks clean comparison: parameter counts of 0.5B-3.8B [17][18][19][20][21], a 781 kB memory footprint at 8-bit [22] and relative reductions of 90-95% [23] all appear in the same artifact pool, alongside a survey that indexes the field's benchmarks [24].
- Serving realities bound algorithmic wins: determinism-preserving batch-invariant kernels are reported to cost more than 2x in latency and up to 74% of serving throughput [4], and token-level routing breaks the single-model assumptions current serving systems are built on [5].

## Background

"Small language model" is used in the retrieved literature for at least two different things. Most commonly it denotes a model in roughly the half-billion to few-billion parameter band - MobiLlama at 0.5B [18], sub-billion MobileLLM [19], a 1B MobileLLM-Pro [20], a 1.7B SmolLM2 [17], Qwen2 spanning 0.5B to 72B [25], and Phi-3-mini at 3.8B [21] - with a survey of the area organizing the field around architectures, training methods and compression techniques [24]. Rarely, it denotes a model whose constraint is memory rather than parameter count, as with a tiny model reporting a 781 kB footprint [22]. The efficiency problem these models serve is physical: weight memory, memory bandwidth, per-token latency, energy and context length all scale with model size and sequence length, and the key-value cache grows with context [14][15]. Three deployment pressures recur across the retrieved work: on-device inference where weights and activations must fit constrained memory and use integer arithmetic [19][22][26], long-context inference where cache memory and bandwidth dominate [14][15], and high-concurrency serving where batch composition decides throughput [27][4][5].

The retrieved methods can be grouped by which quantity they shrink. *Shrinking the model* covers quantization, pruning and distillation. *Shrinking the work per token* covers dynamic-width execution, cache eviction and context compaction. *Shrinking the cost per generated token* covers speculative decoding. *Shrinking the cost per request across models* covers routing and serving-system design. The remaining sections follow this grouping, because the reporting conventions - and therefore the strength of the evidence - differ sharply between them.

## Shrinking the model I: quantization and low-bit artifacts

The two most aggressive quantization results found are both post-training pipelines and both headline bits per scalar rather than task accuracy. HyperQuant is a unified pipeline (Hadamard transform, optimal packing, entropy coding) for the weights and KV cache of language and diffusion transformers; it reports outperforming the HIGGS scheme at every operating point from 3 to 5 bits per scalar on weights and beating TurboQuant and OCTOPUS on KV quantization down to 1.7 bits, and quantizes a 19B video diffusion model at 4 bits end to end on an H100 [1]. Squeeze10-LLM instead frames extreme compression as the target, reporting a 10x squeeze of 16-bit weights to an average of 1.6 bits per weight by quantizing 80% of weights to 1 bit and the remainder to higher precision, with bounded degradation [6].

Artifact-level evidence for low-bit deployment is thinner and differently framed. MobileLLM-Pro reports only minor regressions at 4-bit quantization for a 1B on-device model, achieved with implicit positional distillation and specialist model merging [20]; EmbBERT-Q reports a 781 kB memory footprint at 8-bit for embedded NLP [22]; MobileQuant uses integer-only quantization to reduce latency and energy on mobile devices [26]. A third philosophy appears in Matryoshka Quantization, which makes a single model servable at multiple precision levels and reports improved int2 accuracy - i.e. it optimizes accuracy at a fixed low bit-width instead of choosing one operating point in advance [28].

The comparison is therefore not a single ranking but a difference in what each line optimizes. HyperQuant and Squeeze10-LLM push the achievable bit-width down while naming the baselines they beat [1][6]; Matryoshka Quantization treats precision as a per-request serving decision [28]; MobileQuant and EmbBERT-Q optimize hardware constraints (integer-only arithmetic, footprint) rather than bit-width per se [26][22]; MobileLLM-Pro is one of the few artifacts that reports a quantization-induced quality delta on a deployed-scale checkpoint [20]. The common limitation is reporting shape: the retrieved abstracts give bits per scalar, named comparisons and footprint figures, but not full accuracy-versus-latency curves, so the quality price of the most extreme points in [1] and [6] cannot be read off this evidence.

## Shrinking the model II: distillation and pruning

Distillation in the retrieved set splits by scope. SQuaD-SQL is task-scoped: LLM-guided knowledge distillation lets small models approach large-model performance on Text-to-SQL while reducing the compute that hampers deployment in resource-constrained settings [7]. Chain-based Distillation is family-scoped: it initializes variable-sized small models by stepwise distillation through a sparse sequence of intermediate anchor models, targeting the cost of training small models from scratch and the poor scalability of conventional distillation that needs repeated access to a large teacher for every target size [10]. Two further entries optimize the distillation process itself rather than the student: MiniLLM distills generative language models with reverse KLD for better precision and quality [8], and a later pipeline caches teacher top-K logits offline and uses a memory-linear fused chunked KL loss, reported to enable longer contexts and faster training [9].

Pruning splits along the accuracy-versus-realized-speedup fault line. Correlation-aware structured pruning argues that existing importance criteria score channels or heads in isolation, implicitly assuming pruning errors are additive - an assumption invalidated by non-orthogonal weights and correlated activations - and reformulates pruning to account for those correlations while keeping hardware-friendly structure [11]. WIDE argues from the opposite direction: static structured pruning is hardware-friendly and yields real throughput gains but is input-agnostic and degrades accuracy under aggressive sparsity, whereas existing dynamic-sparsity methods make only coarse structural decisions and rarely convert sparsity into practical acceleration; WIDE therefore performs token-level dynamic width pruning [2]. Adapt-Pruner combines layer-wise adaptive pruning with further training and reports maintaining or even improving performance relative to the pre-trained model [12], while DarwinLM uses evolutionary, training-aware structured pruning and reports state-of-the-art compression with reduced post-compression training data [13]. Super Tiny Language Models reduces parameter counts by 90-95% through byte-level tokenization, weight tying and efficient training rather than pruning at all [23].

Read together, structured and training-aware pruning optimizes for deployable structure and pays with post-compression training [11][12][13], while dynamic-width pruning optimizes for quality under high sparsity and takes on the harder problem of realizing the theoretical speedup in hardware [2]. Distillation differs from pruning in kind rather than degree: it can target a capability or task [7][8], it can be used to manufacture a whole family of sizes from one teacher lineage [10], and its own compute cost has become an object of optimization [9] - whereas pruning starts from an existing model whose behaviour it must preserve [12][13].

## Small models by construction: architecture, training and scale

The largest block of retrieved artifacts are small models whose efficiency comes from architecture and pre-training decisions rather than post-hoc compression. MobileLLM argues for deep, thin architectures in the sub-billion regime for on-device use [19], and MobileLLM-Pro extends that line to a 1B model with long context and 4-bit-friendly behaviour [20]. MobiLlama reaches 0.5B with parameter sharing to balance accuracy and efficiency for resource-constrained devices [18]. PLM is explicitly hardware co-designed, using multi-head latent attention and squared ReLU activation to cut storage and bandwidth for edge devices [29]. Phi-4-Mini takes the adapter route, using a mixture of LoRA modules to make compact language and multimodal models [30], while SmolVLM applies the same compactness goal multimodally with minimal GPU memory for mobile and edge deployment [31]. PhoneLM uses architecture search to find an on-device family with a reported state-of-the-art capability-efficiency balance [32], and SlimLM ships an on-device document-assistant series together with a benchmark for on-device language models [33].

Training-side strategies are the second axis. SmolLM2 attributes its 1.7B model's strong performance to overtraining on diverse data [17], MiniCPM pairs scaling techniques with learning-rate management to make small models comparable to larger ones [34], and Qwen2 is the broadest size range found, spanning 0.5B to 72B in one family - which is useful here as a reminder that "small" is the low end of a continuum rather than a separate species [25]. Applications close the loop: an edge function-calling framework built on small models is reported to reach competitive performance against much larger hosted models [35], which is the clearest statement in this evidence set of why capability-per-parameter rather than absolute capability is the right small-model metric. The differences worth noting are architectural rather than stylistic: depth-over-width [19][20], parameter sharing [18], latent attention [29], adapter composition [30], architectural search [32] and data scaling [34][17] are competing answers to the same budget question, and the retrieved summaries report them as alternative routes to comparable capability rather than as a ranked ladder.

## Data and evaluation substrate for tiny models

Tiny-model efficiency claims are cheap to falsify only if tiny-model training and evaluation data exist, and the retrieved artifact pool contains a clear lineage for exactly that. TinyStories introduces a synthetic story distribution that enables small or simple models to produce fluent, coherent text with reasoning ability, plus an evaluation framework based on human-like grading [36]. SimpleStories scales and parametrizes the recipe, providing 2 million stories each in English and Japanese with controllable prompt characteristics, explicitly addressing limitations of the earlier work [37]. Multilingual TinyStories extends the same idea to 17 Indian languages in a large-scale synthetic children's-story corpus designed for training and evaluating small language models [38]. The trajectory is from a single demonstration that tiny models can be coherent [36] to controllable, multilingual, large-scale corpora [37][38], and the survey entry that indexes architectures, compression techniques, benchmark datasets and metrics provides the broader map into which these corpora fit [24]. Practically, these datasets matter to efficiency research because they let a compression or pruning recipe be validated on a task whose ceiling is known, rather than inferred from a borrowed large-model benchmark.

## Runtime efficiency I: speculative decoding and cache economy

Speculative decoding in this evidence set is no longer arguing for its own premise; it is arguing about which drafting regime survives serving constraints. SEED drafts tokens from the target model itself through an implicit encoder-decoder, explicitly framing the draft-quality-versus-cost trade-off: early-exit drafting is cheap but forgoes deeper representations and hurts draft quality, whereas multi-token prediction preserves quality but pays a full forward pass at each drafting step [3]. DSpark targets the high-concurrency regime, where parallel (semi-autoregressive) drafters suffer rapid acceptance decay from missing inter-token dependencies and where indiscriminately verifying long blocks wastes batch capacity on high-rejection tokens [27]. SpecFold moves the same idea to diffusion language models, verifying a main branch together with multiple draft branches in one forward pass for iterative block denoising, and identifies multi-branch computational redundancy as an axis complementary to the temporal redundancy across denoising steps that earlier acceleration exploited [39].

The cache-and-context side trades information for memory instead of buying speed with parallel work. CORE starts from an exact factorization in which eviction error equals the evicted attention mass multiplied by the directional gap between the evicted centroid and the retained output, motivating set-level coverage in retention and mass-preserving writes, and contrasting itself with fixed-budget methods that separate retention from compensation [14]. Distance-KV observes that retrieval capability varies substantially with relative distance even within a single attention head, and learns a static retention pattern offline over the joint space of layers, heads and relative distances [15]. REMORY addresses agentic contexts directly: when history must be compacted to continue inside a finite window, a textual summary alone may not support every later decision, so it augments the summary with a bounded sequence of soft memory tokens, appended like a residual along the sequence dimension, to help a frozen model approximate the continuation it would have produced with full history [16].

Comparatively, speculative decoding spends extra compute to reduce the number of sequential model calls while preserving the verified output [3][27][39], whereas eviction and compaction reduce memory and per-step context cost while explicitly discarding the possibility of attending to everything [14][15][16]. The two are complementary in principle but the retrieved evidence tests them separately, and the only cross-cutting observation available is a coverage one: in the daily-paper slice sampled for this survey, decoding acceleration and context compaction were returned as efficiency items [39][16], while explicit quantization, on-device and compression vocabulary returned nothing - so the visible community signal skews to runtime.

## Runtime efficiency II: serving systems and the price of guarantees

Serving-system work in this evidence set exists largely to price the assumptions the algorithm papers leave implicit. CoRun targets output nondeterminism caused by batch-dependent GPU execution, where dynamic input shapes alter kernel tiling and floating-point reduction order; it reports that existing batch-invariant kernels increase latency by more than 2x and reduce serving throughput by up to 74%, and proposes padding as a simpler and cheaper route to determinism [4]. TokenRouter argues that token-level routing across models can move the cost-quality Pareto frontier substantially beyond the widely adopted session- or query-level routing, but that existing systems, which assume a single LLM, suffer step desynchronization and frequent batch-admission problems when serving it [5]. DSpark makes the same point from the decoding side, noting that verifying low-acceptance blocks degrades throughput specifically in high-concurrency serving [27].

The contrast with the compression sections is the point: quantization, pruning and distillation report per-model metrics - bits per scalar, parameter reduction, retained accuracy [1][6][11][23] - while the serving papers show that a fraction of those wins can be consumed by system-level requirements such as determinism or by architectural mismatches such as multi-model routing [4][5]. Conversely, the runtime methods that do report serving-regime awareness do so as a first-class design constraint rather than an afterthought [27][5]. No retrieved work measures compression and adaptive runtime methods inside one serving stack, which is the clearest structural gap in this evidence set.

## Trends and open problems

**Convergence on adaptive, structure-aware, system-aware methods.** The strongest retrieved methods reject uniform treatment of their object: pruning accounts for correlations between units instead of scoring them in isolation [11], width becomes a per-token decision rather than a static mask [2], KV retention becomes a function of distance rather than a fixed budget [15], speculative decoding becomes confidence-scheduled rather than uniformly block-based [27], and routing becomes token-level rather than query-level [5]. The consistent failure mode being repaired is the same one in each case: a static, coarse or context-blind prior whose error shows up only under distribution shift or load.

**Evidence asymmetry between fronts.** Compression work abstracts well into bit-widths and named baseline comparisons [1][6], and its quality cost is usually characterized relative to a pre-compression baseline [6][12][13], while serving work abstracts into latency and throughput deltas [4][5] and artifact work into parameter counts and absolute benchmarks [21][25]. Because the retrieved abstracts rarely carry complete accuracy-versus-latency curves and never compare across categories, claims that one lever dominates quality-per-cost are defensible only within a cluster, not across them.

**A reporting-standard gap around size.** Parameter counts of 0.5B, 1B, 1.7B and 3.8B [17][18][20][21], a 781 kB footprint [22], a 90-95% relative reduction [23] and two entries with no numeric size at all in their retrieved summaries [34][30] are being compared in practice as if they measured the same quantity. A shared reporting convention - parameters, weight footprint at a stated precision, and context length together - would make artifact comparisons meaningful, and the survey entry covering architectures, compression and benchmarks is the natural place to anchor it [24].

**Deployment and evaluation gaps.** In the search slice sampled here, small-model artifacts were returned as paper entries rather than checkpoint cards, so the availability of ready-to-serve compressed checkpoints is not verifiable from this evidence even where 4-bit and 8-bit behaviour is reported [20][22][28]. Likewise, no retrieved artifact supplies a full benchmark table for the smallest class of models, which makes the aggressive reductions reported elsewhere [1][6][23] hard to place on a capability map; the recent extension of tiny-model corpora to new languages and controllable generation is a partial remedy because it supplies a shared, known-difficulty substrate [36][37][38].

**Open problems that follow directly.** First, guarantees are now priced but not yet integrated: determinism has an explicit latency and throughput cost [4], yet no retrieved method evaluates whether aggressive compression [1][6] changes that cost or interacts with adaptive runtime decisions [2][27]. Second, the interaction between caching policy and speculative decoding is untested in this evidence set, even though both are deployed in the same inference loop [15][3][27]. Third, small-model demand is being met by both compressed large models and purpose-built small ones [7][34][31][19][33][32][29][35] without a shared evaluation protocol that would say which is cheaper per unit of capability under a given memory and latency budget - the most consequential unanswered question in this survey.

**A note on evidence provenance.** Every source in this survey was returned by the host research tools used to build it (the arXiv search tool, the Hugging Face Daily Papers tool and Hugging Face search), and each numeric claim above is recorded as the retrieved abstract or summary states it. Independent re-fetching of the cited URLs was attempted for a spot-check subsample but failed in this run because the fetch service returned HTTP 429 on every attempt, so no claim here has been independently re-confirmed against the live pages; the extreme-compression bit-widths, the throughput and latency deltas and the reported footprint figures should therefore be treated as author-reported numbers pending such confirmation.
