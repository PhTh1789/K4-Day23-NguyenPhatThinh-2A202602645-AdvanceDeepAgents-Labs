# Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning: Algorithms, Credit Assignment, Rewards, and Evidence

## TL;DR

- Critic-free, group-relative policy optimization has become the default recipe for training reasoning models: GRPO removes the need for a critic network of the same size as the policy by using group scores as the baseline [1], practitioner libraries expose GRPO, Dr. GRPO, RLOO and PPO as one-flag alternatives [2][3], and large-scale RL is credited with o1's reasoning ability, with performance reported to improve with both more train-time and more test-time compute [4].
- The most consistently reported weakness of that recipe is credit assignment: a single trajectory-level advantage is broadcast uniformly over all tokens, which independent recent papers characterise as indiscriminate [5], polarity-misaligned [6] and ceiling-imposing [7].
- Reward design is a trade-off between sparse-but-exact outcome verification and dense-but-noisy learned step feedback [8]; the strongest measured configurations mix deterministic verifiers with a learned or co-trained model [9][10].
- Reported instabilities have identifiable mechanisms — reliance on importance sampling [11], clipping-induced gradient starvation under rollout reuse [12], and late-stage contraction of effective strategy capacity [13] — and the engineering layer answers them with asynchronous rollout servers, partial-rollout training and off-policy correction [14][15][2].
- Whether RL expands reasoning ability beyond the base model is contested: one line of work argues RLVR mainly re-weights the output distribution toward paths the base model already produces [16], while a standardised re-evaluation reports only limited gains over supervised fine-tuning [17].
- Evaluation and data infrastructure lag the algorithms: benchmark suites now explicitly target the "beyond the base model" question [18], a lineage audit finds heavy overlap and contamination risk in RLVR datasets [19], and process reward models are still weak outside mathematics [20].

## Background

Reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning denotes post-training of a language model with a reward signal derived from whether its final answer (or its proof, or its executed code) is verifiable, with the aim of improving multi-step chain-of-thought behaviour. Surveys of the area organise the field around PPO, Q-learning and actor-critic families while grouping RLHF, RLAIF, DPO and GRPO as the dominant post-training algorithms [21], and a unifying analysis recasts on-policy RL and offline preference optimisation as two views of the same neural structured bandit problem [22]. The modern instantiation of the recipe is DeepSeekMath's GRPO [1]; the disclosure of DeepSeek-R1 and its RL-only R1-Zero ablation [23][24], together with OpenAI's description of o1 as large-scale RL [4], moved the paradigm from a niche RLHF variant to the centre of reasoning-model development.

A structural feature of the field is that capability claims outrun published artefacts: the replication effort around R1 notes that no training code was released, so which hyperparameters work best across model families and scales is unknown [25], and later standardised evaluation work identifies transparency and run-to-run variability as first-order problems for interpreting reported gains [17]. Nearly all sources surveyed here therefore report results against their own recipes rather than a shared, seeded protocol [25][17].

## Algorithm families: from actor–critic PPO to critic-free objectives

The actor–critic lineage retains a learned value function and treats credit assignment as the object of design: PACT proves that three regularity conditions — Completeness, Prefix Consistency and Neutrality — uniquely determine token-level credit, and uses that characterisation to construct an improved actor–critic training procedure [26]. The classical survey framing places PPO and actor–critic methods at the core of the field [21]. The countervailing argument is cost: a critic of the same size as the policy "introduces significant computational and memory overhead" [27], and removing it is exactly what GRPO does [1].

Within the critic-free family the disagreement is no longer whether to keep a critic but how to compute the advantage. REINFORCE++ argues that the prompt-level (local) advantage normalisation used by GRPO and RLOO is a theoretically biased estimator that promotes overfitting, and substitutes global normalisation [27]; Bellman Policy Optimization derives an alternative critic-free rule from policy mirror descent via the Bellman equations and proves the same unique optimum as the original objective [28]; PS-PPO accepts critic-free training as the practical alternative to actor–critic training but faults it for propagating a single trajectory-level signal across every token and for requiring full-trajectory updates, proposing prefix sampling instead [29]. At the variant-engineering level, DAPO's dynamic sampling filters prompts whose sampled responses are entirely correct or entirely incorrect, and the paper's own analysis attributes most of the accuracy gain over GRPO to that component while noting a drawback relative to plain GRPO [30]; DAPO is also presented as an open-source system at scale explicitly framed around reproducibility [31]. GVPO++ attacks instability by folding the analytical solution of KL-constrained reward maximisation into the gradient weighting scheme [11].

Offline preference optimisation shares the goal but not the machinery. DVO drops preference labels entirely and supervises individual reasoning steps with value targets estimated by Monte Carlo tree search or an outcome value model [32]. Its advocates and its critics agree on the cost argument — DPO needs no reward model and no online sampling [33] — while the bidirectional-negative-feedback work reports that DPO and its variants remain hyperparameter-sensitive and unstable "particularly on mathematical datasets" [33], and hard-negative sampling work adds that pipelines depending on large reward models or LLM-as-a-judge signals are expensive, difficult to scale and unstable to iterate [34]. The unification view is that these are not rival paradigms but different parameterisations of one RLHF objective [22].

Iterative and self-taught variants close the loop between generation and training. RL-STaR provides a theoretical analysis of STaR-style frameworks in which the model generates its own reasoning steps to reduce dependence on human-annotated rationales [35]. ASTRO trains models to reflect, backtrack and explore in-context, but cautions that open replications typically start from base models that already exhibit strong reasoning and search behaviour before RL, which clouds attribution of reported gains [36]. Because verifiable rewards are confined to domains with deterministic checking, the RLSVR line transforms open-ended tasks so that rewards become self-verifiable rather than outsourcing evaluation to human preferences or judges that bring evaluation bias, judge-capability bottlenecks and extra inference cost [37]; PACS takes the opposite tack of recasting RLVR as a supervised learning problem for stability and efficiency [38].

The engineering layer has largely converged on this menu. OpenRLHF's documentation lists PPO, REINFORCE++, REINFORCE++-baseline, GRPO, Dr. GRPO and RLOO as switchable with one flag [2]; TRL separates online trainers (GRPO, RLOO, and experimental asynchronous or GMPO variants), reward modelling, and offline methods [3]; NeMo RL lists GRPO, PPO, SFT, DPO, reward modelling, on-policy distillation and multi-teacher on-policy distillation [39].

## Credit assignment: the central technical bottleneck

The single most repeated technical criticism across the surveyed literature is that outcome-level, group-relative advantage treats every token in a rollout identically. GRPO is described as achieving critic-free training at the price of "indiscriminate credit assignment for intermediate steps", which is said to limit identification of effective reasoning strategies and to encourage overthinking [5]. EP-GRPO enumerates three distinct failures: uniform token-level granularity that ignores heterogeneous informational value, uniform polarity that "penalizes correct steps and rewards incorrect ones", and zero-variance collapse that erases outcome-driven gradients [6]. FIPO argues that distributing a global advantage uniformly imposes a performance ceiling by failing to distinguish critical logical pivots from trivial tokens [7]; a hierarchical-credit paper says refinements based only on local signals such as token position or entropy miss the global semantic novelty of a reasoning behaviour [40]; and PS-PPO makes the same complaint from the systems side [29].

Proposed remedies differ mainly in what they use as a denser signal. GRPO-VPS adds process supervision without training a step reward model by probing the model's own belief in the correct answer [5]. EP-GRPO aligns credit with entropy progress [6]. FIPO injects a discounted future-KL divergence term into the policy update [7]. The hierarchical scheme compares reasoning behaviours using fused Gromov–Wasserstein geometry [40]. A span-level approach extracts Wasserstein distances between hidden-state distributions within each GRPO group, using only the outcome-level correctness labels that RLVR already provides — denser credit without step annotations, a process reward model, or any additional reward modelling [41]. PACT and Bellman Policy Optimization attack the same problem from the theory side by deriving what token-level or critic-free credit should be rather than heuristically re-weighting it [26][28].

A second cluster of work asks why these objectives become unstable rather than how to sharpen the signal. GVPO++ attributes GRPO-style instability specifically to reliance on importance sampling [11]. ReSPO identifies "sign-dependent gradient starvation" in clipped policy optimisation under RLVR rollout reuse: clipping suppresses under-generated positive responses at the low importance-weight tail while letting severely over-generated negative responses dominate the high-weight tail, and it replaces clipping with a two-branch sequence-level kernel derived from an α-divergence variational objective, reporting larger gains as the number of sampled responses grows and better behaviour on dense and MoE Qwen3 models under high staleness [12]. A theoretical RLVR study reports severe late-stage collapse and shows, via prompt-based probing, that this is "not benign strategic pruning, but a harmful contraction of effective strategy capacity" [13]. Entropy-guided credit assignment is offered with an explicit caveat: prioritising uncertain positions under both reinforcement and penalisation concentrates penalties where failed responses still retain alternatives for recovery, suppressing exploration [42]. Complementary geometric evidence suggests RL-induced gains live on a low-dimensional effective manifold in activation space whose control directions sit mainly in the low-variance complement of the principal subspace; the Alpha-Stabler remedy monitors principal-subspace intrusion for early collapse warnings and strips the principal-subspace component of activation gradients in backpropagation, reported to stabilise training for 2,000 steps across five LLMs and six verifiable-reward tasks [43].

## Reward and verifier design

The core reward-design tension is stated crisply in the process-reward literature: pipelines that "optimize outcome correctness only" give sparse feedback for long multi-step solutions and little guidance on intermediate errors, while in practice process reward model scores are "often imperfectly aligned with final correctness" [8]. Evidence for the value of density is mixed in an informative way. A hybrid-reward study reports that when reward is sparse it is better to be dense, combining verifier signals with reward-model scores and outperforming either alone [9]; RL Tango goes further and concurrently trains a generative model and an RL-trained verifier, reporting better robustness and out-of-domain generalisation [10]; General-Reasoner uses generative model-based answer verification at scale [44]. On the noise side, a data-scaling study of RLHF identifies reward hacking and decreasing response diversity as the principal data-driven bottlenecks and proposes a hybrid system that pairs reasoning-task verifiers with a generative reward model to mitigate hacking [45]. A concrete negative result exists for deterministic guidance: once denoising, PRM scoring and outcome-reward-model scoring are all charged to the same forward-pass budget, deterministic PRM guidance loses to a much simpler baseline on Dream-v0-Instruct-7B with 8 candidates per GSM8K problem [46]. Step-level supervision is also being automated rather than annotated, as in multimodal step-level annotation pipelines [47], and dedicated evaluation shows PRMs struggle to detect errors outside mathematics, where knowledge-based and computational errors behave differently across model types [20].

Length and verbosity have become a reward-design lever in their own right, with openly conflicting choices. At frontier scale, groupwise agentic grading is claimed to yield more accurate reward signals for long-horizon tasks while steering the model toward shorter, more token-efficient solutions, alongside a frozen MoE router and a multi-layer defence against reward hacking [48]. Elsewhere, up-weighting moderately easy problems in math RLVR is reported to reduce verbosity without any explicit length penalty [49], whereas ReSPO's positive branch deliberately preserves gradient weight for long, under-generated positive responses [12]. The reward-plumbing interfaces that support such choices are now explicit and testable: NeMo RL environments wrap a math verifier and sandboxed code execution and return either a single `(batch_size,)` reward or a `(batch_size, num_components)` tensor exposed as per-component rewards for multi-objective baselines [50]; TRL ships an `accuracy_reward` that returns `None` to skip examples whose gold answer is unparseable and a `reasoning_accuracy_reward` that strips everything before user-specified reasoning delimiters and verifies only the remainder [51]; and QwQ-32B's stage-1 recipe replaced a reward model entirely with an accuracy verifier for math plus a code execution server, adding a general reward model and rule-based verifiers only in stage 2 [52].

Verifiability also bounds where the paradigm applies. The RLSVR argument that RLVR is largely limited to deterministic verification [37] is consistent with the pattern of published domains: a 3B medical model shows in-distribution effectiveness and out-of-distribution generalisation from RLVR without explicit reasoning supervision [53], and formal mathematics supplies the cleanest verifiers of all, with Lean-based benchmarks of formalised problems [54], a 560-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts prover trained with agentic tool-integrated RL and hierarchical policy optimisation for long-horizon stability [55], a 7B prover trained on verifier feedback [56], and self-verifying theorem-proving systems that reward rigorous step-by-step derivations [57]. Search-augmented RLVR integrates Monte Carlo tree search into training for exploration and credit assignment and reports state-of-the-art results at reduced computational cost [58].

## Training dynamics, scaling and the infrastructure layer

Scale figures in the surveyed material are concrete but sparse. An asynchronous RL setup at the frontier consumes 1,568 samples and 2.7–3.7B tokens per step at context lengths of up to 1M tokens, with RL compute scaled along batch/throughput, environment diversity (code, general, visual, cyber, under a mixture of agent harnesses) and grader compute [48]. On the systems side, a fully asynchronous PPO recipe decoupling trainer and rollouter reports a 2.35x–2.67x speedup when training Qwen2.5-7B on 128 GPUs "without significantly affecting the results", and supports asynchronous, streaming and partial-rollout training [14]; the same project's release notes enumerate RL pipelines for on-policy, one-step-off-policy and fully asynchronous training and list a checkpoint engine for weight synchronisation among the core components [15]. A reproducible single-node setting is also published: a three-stage context curriculum (8K → 16K → 24K) for GRPO on a 1.5B distilled model, 240/290/50 steps per stage on one 8xH100 node, reaching an average training reward of 0.65 in 400 steps [59]. Exploration-side scaling is addressed by difficulty-adaptive rollout sampling combined with large batches, reported to improve both Pass@K and Pass@1 [60].

The practitioner layer documents failure modes that are invisible in algorithm papers. Pointing an asynchronous GRPO trainer at the wrong environment stack breaks training: it requires a recent vLLM, supports only FSDP2 for distributed training (not DeepSpeed ZeRO), demands picklable reward functions and tools because they cross a process boundary, and runs the rollout process with `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=""`, so a GPU-backed reward model silently falls back to CPU [61]. A major framework abandoned the chat-completion API in favour of a token-level generate interface, stating that "the conversion between tokens and text can be irreversible" [62]. Another ships explicit off-policy correction methods for vLLM-to-trainer log-probability mismatch [2]. Underneath all of this sits a reproducibility caveat from an independent primary source: LLM inference is not deterministic even at temperature 0 in hosted APIs and in widely used open-source inference libraries, and the usual concurrency-plus-floating-point explanation is incomplete — which directly undermines treating rollout log probabilities as reproducible quantities during training [63].

The framework layer itself has consolidated around hybrid controller designs that decouple training backends from inference engines [64], GRPO configuration semantics that fix group sampling size, mini-batch structure and clipping ratios as exposed knobs [65], a documented minimal GRPO recipe (small instruct model plus a verifiable-reward dataset, launched on 8 GPUs in roughly a day) [66], memory-oriented optimisations such as fused language-model heads reported to give up to 6.9x longer sequences and 52%–82% lower peak VRAM at 8k tokens [3], and trainer suites that span GRPO through speculative-decoding-accelerated rollout [39]. Representation-level efficiency is not settled either: a comparative evaluation of more than twelve parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods under RLVR on distilled reasoning models reports that the optimal PEFT architecture remains unidentified, challenging default LoRA adoption [67]. Finally, distillation is a distinct transfer channel with an asymmetric profile — reverse-KL on-policy distillation reliably transfers compositional skill across unseen reasoning structures but minimal factual knowledge, forward KL restores factual transfer, and the headline conclusion is that such distillation reorganises existing knowledge rather than expanding parametric knowledge [68].

## Data and artifact ecosystem

Published data resources give the paradigm its raw material, and their scales are among the few hard numbers available: a competition-winning recipe reports 540K unique high-quality math problems including olympiad-level items, 3.2M long-reasoning solutions and 1.7M tool-integrated solutions obtained via code execution [69]; a reinforcement-learning-specific corpus exceeds 250,000 math questions spanning domains and difficulty levels [70]; a decontaminated, verifiable math dataset is offered explicitly to advance RL-driven reasoning [71]; and open data recipes are reported to yield reasoning models matching or exceeding state-of-the-art benchmarks in math, code and science [72]. Against this, a lineage-tracing audit of RLVR datasets reports heavy overlap and contamination risk across widely used sources [19], which matters directly for interpreting the capability claims in the previous sections.

Artifact disclosures vary sharply in completeness. DeepSeek-R1's multi-stage training is reported to deliver performance comparable to a contemporaneous closed reasoning model [23], and its RL-only variant validates that reasoning capability can be incentivised purely through RL, at the cost of endless repetition, poor readability and language mixing — the reported motivation for adding cold-start data and for a pipeline of two RL stages plus two SFT stages [24]. QwQ-32B documents verifier-based rather than reward-model-based outcome RL [52], and the surrounding replication ecosystem has had to fill the documentation gap left by frontier labs [25].

## Evaluation: benchmarks and contested capability claims

Benchmark practice in this area splits into two philosophies. The first asks whether RL moves the capability boundary at all: one benchmark is constructed explicitly to require reasoning beyond the current model's capabilities and thus to test whether RL expands beyond the base model [18]; critical analyses of R1-Zero-like training show that pretraining differences confound many apparent RL gains [73]; a standardised re-evaluation framework reports limited improvement over supervised fine-tuning on mathematical benchmarks together with transparency and variability problems [17]; and a distributional argument holds that RLVR biases the output distribution toward already-reachable rewarded paths without expanding the reasoning boundary [16]. The second philosophy tracks headroom on hard, contamination-resistant problems: an IMO-level high-school competition benchmark reports that current models still have significant room for improvement [74], and a parallel English/Chinese Olympiad-level benchmark serves the same purpose [75]. Both are constrained by measurement limits the community has only begun to address — process reward models remain hard to score outside mathematics [20], lineage audits expose heavy overlap between RLVR corpora [19], and exhaustive comparisons of parameter-efficient RLVR methods find that no architecture is optimal across settings, so even the tuning configuration interacts with reported outcomes [67].

## Trends and open problems

Three trends are visible across the surveyed sources. First, the frontier has moved from single-turn verifiable-reward RL to asynchronous, agentic and long-horizon training: environment mixtures, grader compute and anti-reward-hacking defences are now first-class design objects [48], and rollout infrastructure is being rebuilt around asynchronous serving servers and cross-node parameter synchronisation [14][15][62]. Second, reward construction is converging on mixtures rather than single signals — verifiers plus generative reward models [45][9], co-trained generators and verifiers [10], and per-component multi-objective rewards exposed as first-class API shapes [50] — because neither sparse outcome rewards nor learned step rewards is individually reliable [8][46]. Third, formal and domain-specific verification is expanding the paradigm into mathematics and medicine where correctness is machine-checkable [54][55][56][57][53], while open-ended tasks require task transformation or judges with documented biases and costs [37].

The open problems are equally consistent. Credit assignment remains the field's central theoretical gap: the conditions under which dense, non-annotated signals (belief probes, hidden-state distances, future-KL terms) yield genuine improvements rather than re-weighted noise are not settled [5][7][41], and the two theoretically grounded alternatives disagree about whether a critic should be kept at all [26][28]. Whether RL expands capability or merely sharpens sampling remains actively contested, with benchmark suites constructed specifically to adjudicate it [16][17][18][73]. Process reward models are demonstrably domain-limited and their evaluation is only now being formalised [20], while their deterministic use can underperform simpler baselines at equal compute [46]. Data provenance and contamination are unresolved prerequisites for trusting any of the reported numbers [19], reproducibility is compromised by unseeded nondeterminism in the inference stacks that generate rollouts [63], and transparency about recipes remains partial [25][17]. Finally, verbosity and length control lack a coherent account: the same community reports steering toward shorter solutions [48], implicitly regularising length by data re-weighting [49], and deliberately preserving long positive trajectories [12] within the same outcome-reward paradigm.
